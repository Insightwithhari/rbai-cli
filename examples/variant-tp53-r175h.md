## Part of the answer

The answer is shortened here. It also had sections on what the change does to p53 and what it means for Li-Fraumeni
syndrome, and two more practical points, on treatment planning and on relatives.

> **TP53 p.Arg175His (R175H; NM_000546.6:c.524G>A)** is a well-established pathogenic variant associated with
> Li-Fraumeni syndrome (LFS), also described more broadly as a *heritable TP53-related cancer syndrome*. ClinVar
> lists it as **germline pathogenic**, with a three-star expert-panel review; it is also a recurrent
> cancer-associated mutation when found somatically in a tumor.
> [ClinVar record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/variation/12374)
>
> ### Practical implications
>
> - **Confirm whether a finding is germline.** A tumor-only R175H result does not establish LFS: TP53 mutations are
>   common in cancers, and blood-based results can sometimes be complicated by clonal hematopoiesis or mosaicism. A
>   genetics team can select appropriate confirmatory testing.
> - **If germline, discuss surveillance with an LFS-experienced team.** Protocols commonly include regular clinical
>   assessment and imaging such as whole-body MRI; age- and sex-specific screening is also used. Surveillance is
>   intended to detect cancers early and should be tailored to current guidance and the person’s circumstances.
>   [Achatz et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-24-3301)
>
> **Bottom line:** R175H is a strongly established disease-causing TP53 variant when germline. It signals a need for
> expert genetic counseling and LFS-oriented care, but it does not determine an individual’s exact cancer risks or
> outcomes.

The other sources it linked were [Bougeard et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26014290/) and the
