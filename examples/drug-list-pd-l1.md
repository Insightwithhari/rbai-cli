## Part of the answer

The answer is shortened here. It also listed four antibodies approved in China and other countries.

> **Direct PD‑L1 inhibitors** include these therapeutic antibodies:
>
> **Approved in the U.S.**
>
> - **Atezolizumab** (Tecentriq)
> - **Avelumab** (Bavencio)
> - **Durvalumab** (Imfinzi)
> - **Cosibelimab** (Unloxcyt) — approved for certain advanced cutaneous squamous-cell cancers in 2024.
>   [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-cosibelimab-ipdl-metastatic-or-locally-advanced-cutaneous-squamous-cell-carcinoma)
>
> **Investigational or preclinical examples**
>
> - **INCB086550**, **INCB099280** and **INCB099318** — oral small-molecule PD‑L1 inhibitors evaluated in clinical
>   studies. [Clinical characterization of INCB086550](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35254416/)
> - **BMS‑202** and **BMS‑1166** — commonly studied experimental small molecules, not approved medicines.
>   [Small-molecule PD‑L1 inhibitor review](https://doi.org/10.1039/d3md00655g)
> - **CA‑170** — reported as a PD‑L1/VISTA-pathway candidate, but direct binding to PD‑L1 has been disputed, so it
>   should not be treated as an established direct PD‑L1 inhibitor.
>   [Evidence review](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules24152804)
>
> This is a practical list of prominent agents, not every compound reported in discovery research. It excludes
> **PD‑1 inhibitors** such as pembrolizumab and nivolumab, which target the receptor rather than PD‑L1.
